# Content Patterns (from eSafety + Thorn)

Status: reference-derived guidance
Last updated: 2026-08-05
Sources: `reference/esafety-crawl.md`, `reference/thorn-org-crawl.md`.

These are the transferable patterns from eSafety (the closest live model to Asmita)
and Thorn. Structure and tone only. All legal content is Australian/US and must be
rewritten for Indian law. All copy is rewritten in Asmita's voice, never lifted.

## 1. The four-step spine (name it once, reuse everywhere)

eSafety's entire product is one four-step flow, retold in each audience's voice:

1. **Collect evidence** (told what the form needs, so gathering has a purpose)
2. **Report / take action**
3. **Stop contact, tighten security, prevent re-sharing** (block AFTER evidence)
4. **Get more help** (wellbeing, support)

Asmita has the same underlying flow. We name it once and reuse it across the
report page, the sextortion page, the deepfake page, and each audience page.

## 2. Safety and helplines come before instructions, always

Emergency number first (India: 112), then women's helpline 181, Childline 1098 for
minors, NCRP for cybercrime. On a sextortion page, helplines sit at the top, not the
bottom. "Stay safe" precedes evidence steps. This ordering is a rule, not a choice.

## 3. Page titles and links in the survivor's own voice

Not legal categories. eSafety uses "My nudes have been shared", "Someone is
threatening to share my nudes", "I may be experiencing it". The reader never has to
map their situation to jargon; the title matches the sentence in their head. Audit
Asmita's "Get Help" routes against this: lead with the plain sentence, keep the
formal term (NCII) as the secondary definition.

## 4. Scenario copy beats category copy (highest-value, low-cost)

eSafety's best device: quoted real scenarios each ending "...what do I do?". We need
an **India-specific set**, written for our context:

- "Someone morphed my photo from Instagram into a nude and is sending it around."
- "My ex is threatening to post our private photos to my family's WhatsApp group."
- "A marriage proposal fell through and now someone is sending my photos to relatives."
- "Someone shared a picture of me without my hijab, what do I do?"
- "A fake nude of me is circulating in my college group."
- "Someone is demanding money or they'll leak my photos."

Cover morphing/deepfake, partner betrayal, family-honour exposure, religious modesty,
community/WhatsApp spread, and financial sextortion. Men and boys included as visible
survivors (eSafety deliberately does this).

## 5. Hash/fingerprint framed as harm reduction, reassurance first

eSafety's sentence to measure ours against: "You need a copy of the image or video,
but you don't need to send it to the platform, you can use an online tool to create a
digital 'fingerprint' (or 'hash') instead." The reassurance ("you don't need to send
it") comes before the mechanism. Route adults to StopNCII, under-18 to Take It Down,
and list participating platforms by name so the survivor can judge if it is worth it.

## 6. State outcomes before the survivor acts

eSafety puts "How we can respond" and "Possible outcomes" ahead of the form. Asmita
says plainly what a notice can and cannot achieve before a survivor signs one. This
matches our honesty principle and our "no whole-internet-removal promise" rule.

## 7. Evidence guidance carries its safety limit inline

"Take screenshots... but don't save or share images of anyone under 18." The limit
lives inside the helpful instruction, not in a separate policy page. Asmita has a
stricter rule (no media reaches our server at all) and states it the same way, inline.

## 8. Content warnings on sensitive pages

"Heads up: this page discusses nudes, consent and pressure, which may be distressing."
Short, at the top, before the content.

## 9. Homepage "understand the harm" cards (plain, one sentence each)

eSafety's home has exactly four harm cards, each a single plain-language clause at
roughly grade-6 reading level, with varied CTA verbs ("Learn more", "Find out how").
Asmita's version: NCII, deepfakes/morphing, sextortion, impersonation/doxxing.

## 10. Quick links row (search intent beats IA purity)

A flat list of the terms people actually search ("Sextortion", "Deepfakes",
"Consent", "Doxxing"), each pointing wherever the answer lives regardless of section.
Cheap, and it rescues people who do not know our navigation.

## 11. A page for the person who did it (harm reduction, later)

eSafety has a page for someone who shared an image, concrete steps to limit spread
without excusing the act. Reduces harm through the one person who can act fastest.
Consider for a later phase.

## IA refinement this suggests

Add **"someone helping a survivor"** as an audience alongside survivor / parents /
young people / platforms (a friend or relative is often the one who acts first).
Keep the POCSO branch coming out of the parents and young-people paths.

## Redirect discipline (operational)

eSafety renamed `/key-issues/` to `/key-topics/` and kept redirects rather than break
inbound links. When Asmita's routes move in this redesign, add redirects from the old
paths (`/how-it-works`, `/resources`, `/start`) so nothing 404s.
