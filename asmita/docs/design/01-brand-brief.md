# Asmita Brand Brief

Status: draft for alignment
Owner: Saquib
Last updated: 2026-08-03

This is document 1 of 4 in the redesign. It defines who Asmita is before any
visual or structural decision is made. Everything downstream (sitemap, design
system, page templates) must serve what is written here. Reference feel:
thorn.org, a serious technology-and-research organization, not a charity.

## What Asmita is

A tech-led response platform against non-consensual intimate imagery and related
technology-facilitated abuse in India. We combine working tools (takedown notice
generation and routing, fingerprint blocking, AI/deepfake checking) with a body
of knowledge (plain-language explanations, legal pathways, research, reports).

We help a survivor regain control: preserve evidence, reduce distribution, pursue
removals, secure their accounts, and reach the right support. The tool to file
and route a complaint is the primary action a visitor can take, but it is one
capability inside a larger platform, not the whole website.

## What Asmita is not

- **Not a nonprofit or charity.** No donation asks, no guilt-based messaging, no
  "help us help them" framing, no sponsor-logo walls seeking money. If a line
  could appear on a fundraising page, it does not belong here.
- **Not a government body.** We say so plainly. We route to official channels
  (NCRP, grievance officers, One Stop Centres) but we are independent.
- **Not an NGO front.** Built by JAMSAQ Studio. We do not pretend to be a
  volunteer collective.
- **Not a single-purpose complaint form.** The old site read as one narrow
  intake flow. The new site reads as a platform with an issue, solutions,
  research, and a clear way to act.

## Positioning line

Work-in-progress options (pick one later, in the copy pass):

- "Technology and knowledge to fight image-based abuse in India."
- "Tools and answers for anyone facing non-consensual or AI-made intimate images."
- "A response platform for image-based abuse: report, remove, understand, recover."

## Primary action

Every page ultimately supports one lead action: **Get help / Report.** It is the
most prominent button in the header and the hero. Education and research surround
it and build the authority that makes a scared visitor trust the button, but they
never bury it. Think Thorn's structure, but where our hero action is "get help"
rather than "explore solutions," because our primary visitor is often in crisis.

## Audiences (in priority order)

1. **Survivors and the people helping them.** Often on a cheap Android phone, in
   distress, low patience for jargon. They need calm, fast, obvious paths.
2. **Researchers, press, students, policymakers.** They need credibility:
   terminology, data, reports, methodology, a real team.
3. **Platforms and partners.** They need to see we are legitimate and technical.

The design must serve audience 1 first without looking flimsy to audience 2.

## Voice and tone

- **Plain, human, and specific.** "Someone is threatening to leak my photos"
  sits next to the formal term. Short sentences. No legalese unless we then
  explain it.
- **Calm authority, not alarm.** We are serious and factual. We do not use fear,
  urgency banners, or dramatic language to push action.
- **Honest about limits.** We never overpromise ("we will erase it from the whole
  internet"). We say exactly what we can and cannot do. This is a trust asset.
- **Never clinical or cold.** Authoritative does not mean robotic. A survivor
  should feel spoken to like a person.
- **Bilingual by default.** English and Hindi are equal citizens, with more
  Indian languages planned. Copy must translate cleanly and avoid idioms.

Banned in copy: donation language, pity, "empowerment" cliches, stock-inspirational
filler, and anything that reads as marketing fluff over substance.

## Trust signals we will use (Thorn-style, adapted, honest)

We build authority through specifics, not badges we have not earned. Only claim
what is true today:

- Concrete capability statements ("computes fingerprints on your device; the
  server never sees your image").
- Real legal grounding (the actual IT Rules / MeitY SOP pathways).
- Research and reports we actually publish.
- A named, real team and clear "who we are."
- Named infrastructure/partners we genuinely have (e.g. Cloudflare for Startups),
  stated factually, never as endorsement.

Do not fake metrics, partners, or endorsements. If we do not have a number yet,
we do not invent one.

## The "not a nonprofit" test

Before shipping any page, ask: could this be mistaken for a donation site or an
NGO? If yes, fix the copy, the imagery, or the layout until it reads as a
technology-and-response platform. This is a primary acceptance criterion for the
whole redesign.

## Open decisions (resolve during the copy/design pass)

- Final positioning line (three candidates above).
- Whether "Asmita" or "Meri Asmita" is the lead wordmark.
- How prominently JAMSAQ Studio is credited on-site.
- Photography/illustration approach (Thorn uses non-graphic, everyday human
  imagery; we need an approach that is human but never depicts a victim).
